1.4 Finite and Infinite Sets

If there exists a bijection f:A↦Bf: A \mapsto Bf:A↦B, the sets A,BA, BA,B are said to be equinumerous, denoted by A∼BA \sim BA∼B. And equinumerosity is a kind of equivalence relation.

Not all infinite sets are equinumerous. If a set is equinumerous with N\mathbb{N}N, then the set is countably infinite; and both finite and countably infinite sets are countable, otherwise they are uncountable.

The following four propositions are equivalent:

  • AAA is countable
  • AAA is finite, and there exists a bijection f:A→Nf: A \to \mathbb{N}f:A→N

  • There exists an injection g:A→Ng: A \to \mathbb{N}g:A→N

  • There exists a surjection h:N→Ah: \mathbb{N} \to Ah:N→A

1.7 Alphabets and Languages
